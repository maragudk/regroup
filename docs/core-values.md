# Core Values

These are the values of regroup's maintainers. They decide what goes into the software we publish. If a feature conflicts with them, the feature loses.

Regroup is open source. Anyone can host it, and anyone can change it. We can't control either. What we can do is build software that makes these values the easy path, and ask everyone who hosts or changes it to honour them.

Where the interests of one person and the group pull apart, regroup favours the group, and does so gracefully.

## 1. Kindness

Kindness is a design principle, not just a social expectation. The software should make it easy to be kind and awkward to be cruel.

- A group can protect itself from harmful content without waiting for a moderator. Whatever lets it do so must not become a way to silence a member.
- No features that enable pile-ons or public shaming. No downvotes or negative reactions, and no carrying someone's post out of its conversation to mock it. Quoting someone to reply to them is fine.
- Retracting a comment is a graceful act, not a suspicious one. A retracted comment is folded away and marked as retracted by its author. The group can still open it and read it.
- Moderation protects the group. It does not punish individuals. Moderators can hide content and, as a last resort, remove a member. Nobody carries a lasting public mark: no strike counts, no badges of shame, no record that follows them into other groups.

## 2. Trust and transparency

Trust comes from being able to see what happened. Nothing disappears silently. The history of a conversation is a shared asset, and regroup treats it with respect.

- When someone retracts a comment, it is marked as retracted, not deleted. The group's shared context is not rewritten.
- When a moderator takes action, that action is visible. The group can see what happened, who did it, and why.
- Hard deletion is the exception, not the rule. Even then, a note remains that something was removed.
- Mutual accountability: moderators are accountable to the group, and members are accountable for their contributions.

## 3. Respect for attention

Regroup does not compete for your attention. You participate because you want to, on your own schedule.

- No algorithmic feeds, no engagement-optimized notifications, no read receipts, no typing indicators, no presence signals.
- The default notification is a daily digest — a summary that says "things happened, come look when you're ready."
- Users can adjust notification frequency per group: from no emails (check manually) up to immediate notifications. The default is the least intrusive useful option.
- Each person sets this for themselves. The software gives nobody else a way to override it, not even a group admin.
- The pace is closer to a forum or email than to a group chat.
- Nothing in the software gains from holding your attention: it has no advertising and no measure of time spent.

## 4. Community ownership

Content belongs to the group, not to whoever hosts it. Groups are self-governing communities, and regroup is infrastructure for them, not a community itself.

- Group admins can export their group's full history, so a group can leave a host and take its history along.
- The software has no features for using a group's content for anything but showing it to the group: no advertising, no resale, no AI training. We will not add them.
- We ask hosts to take only the permission they need to store content and show it to the group, and to use it for nothing else.
- Members can retract their own contributions, but the group's shared record stays intact.
- Groups set their own norms and manage their own moderation. The software provides the tools, the host sets a baseline of safety, and each community governs itself.

## 5. Privacy without surveillance

Regroup exists because the dominant platforms treat their users as products. Regroup does the opposite.

- The software collects only what it needs to function. It contains no visitor analytics, no behaviour tracking and no advertising features, and we will not add them.
- We ask hosts not to sell members' data, and to share it with others only as far as running the service requires.
- Groups are private. Only members can see a group and what is in it. There is no directory of groups, no discovery algorithm, no trending page, no recommendation engine. You join a group because someone you trust invited you.
