# Core Values

These values guide the design and development of regroup. They are not aspirational — they are constraints. If a feature or decision conflicts with these values, the feature loses.

## 1. Kindness

Kindness is a design principle, not just a social expectation. The software should make it easy to be kind and awkward to be cruel.

- Community-driven flagging can proactively hide harmful content before a moderator intervenes (e.g., multiple flags auto-hide a comment).
- No features that enable pile-ons or public shaming. No quote-dunking, no resharing someone's post to mock it.
- When someone retracts a comment, the platform treats that as a graceful act, not a suspicious one.
- Moderation tools should be effective but not punitive — the goal is to protect the group's wellbeing, not to punish individuals.

## 2. Transparency

Nothing disappears silently. The history of a conversation is a shared asset, and regroup treats it with respect.

- When a user retracts a comment, it is marked as retracted but not deleted. The group's shared context is not rewritten.
- When a moderator takes action, that action is visible — the group can see what happened and why.
- Hard deletion is the exception, not the rule.
- Mutual accountability: moderators are accountable to the group, and members are accountable for their contributions.

## 3. Respect for attention

Regroup does not compete for your attention. You participate because you want to, on your own schedule.

- No algorithmic feeds, no engagement-optimized notifications, no read receipts, no typing indicators, no presence signals.
- The default notification is a daily digest — a summary that says "things happened, come look when you're ready."
- Users can adjust notification frequency per group: from no emails (check manually) up to immediate notifications. The default is the least intrusive useful option.
- The pace is closer to a forum or email than to a group chat.
- Regroup will never make money by maximizing time-on-site, so it has no reason to hijack your attention.

## 4. Community ownership

Content belongs to the group, not to the platform. Groups are self-governing communities, and regroup is infrastructure for them, not a community itself.

- Group admins can export their group's full history.
- The platform claims no license over user-generated content.
- Users can retract their own contributions, but the group's shared reality — the fact that a conversation happened — is respected.
- Groups set their own norms and manage their own moderation. The platform provides tools and a baseline of safety, but each community is self-governing.

## 5. Privacy without surveillance

Regroup exists because the dominant platforms treat their users as products. Regroup does the opposite.

- Collect only what is needed to function. Do not track behavior for advertising. Do not sell or share data with third parties.
- There is no ad-based business model, now or ever.
- Groups can be public or private, but there is no discovery algorithm, no trending page, no recommendation engine. Growth is organic and human — you find a group because someone you trust shared it with you.

## 6. Sustainability without exploitation

However regroup sustains itself, the answer must be compatible with every other value on this list.

- No advertising. No data monetization.
- If it can't be funded honestly, it doesn't get funded.
- Sustainability is itself a kindness — to the people who build it and to the communities who depend on it.
