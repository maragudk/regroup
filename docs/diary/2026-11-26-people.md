# Diary: People: signing in, roles, and invitations

The second piece of work on regroup: what has to exist before anyone but made-up people can use the group page. People sign in with a link sent by email, a group has admins, moderators, and members, and people join by invitation from a moderator or an admin. The requirements are in `/docs/todo/people.md`.

## Step 1: Requirements for the rules and emails

**Author:** main

### Prompt Context

**Verbatim prompt:** "go"
**Interpretation:** Markus's go-ahead on 2026-11-26 for the build order I proposed: the group page with made-up people first, then sign-in, roles, and invitations.
**Inferred intent:** Get to a group that real people can join, without Markus having to settle each detail first. He judges on the preview and overrules what he does not like.

### What I did

The group page merged as pull request #5 (`576ada4`). I removed its worktree and branch, entered a new worktree on `worktree-people`, and wrote the lead's decisions for this piece of work into `/docs/todo/people.md`: how long links last, that a link opens a page with a button, a limit on links per address, that a person joins when they accept, how invitations are replaced and withdrawn, names, who sees email addresses, and that the preview shows emails instead of sending them. I split the work in two, as for the group page: a first builder for the rules and emails with their tests, then a builder for the pages.

### Why

Markus agreed the shape (emailed links, three roles, invitations by moderators and admins) and left the details to me, to be recorded so that he can overrule them. The rules come before the pages because the generated-history tests need the rules callable in-process, and because the pages depend on Markus's verdict on the look of the group page, which he has not given yet.

### What worked

The split used for the group page: data and rules first, pages second, each sized for one builder.

### What didn't work

Nothing failed in this step.

### What I learned

From the group page build: if an invitation created the person's row when it was sent, nothing posted before they accepted would be new to them, and their "joined" position would be wrong. So joining is defined as accepting.

### What was tricky

Deciding what a link in an email does. Mail scanners open links, so a link that signs in when opened would be used up before the person sees it. The link opens a page, and a button on it does the signing in.

### What warrants review

The decisions under "Decided by the lead" in `/docs/todo/people.md` are mine, not Markus's. The ones most likely to be overruled: who sees email addresses, and that names need not be unique.

### Future work

The pages; removing a member, which belongs with moderation; a settings page for admins; and the voice of the emails.

## Step 2: The rules and emails of signing in, roles, and invitations

**Author:** builder

### Prompt Context

**Verbatim prompt:** The lead's brief, which opens: "You are the first builder on regroup's second piece of work: people. Build the rules and emails for signing in, roles, and invitations, with tests. There are no pages in this task; the next builder builds them on what you leave."

### What I did

**Model** (`/model/auth.go`, `/model/people.go`, `/model/error.go`, `/model/jobs.go`): `model.Role` with `RoleAdmin`, `RoleModerator`, and `RoleMember`, and `model.ParseRole`; `model.Action` and `model.Allowed(actor *model.User, action model.Action, target *model.User) bool`, the one table of who may do what, which every operation goes through, the timeline's included; `model.ParseEmailAddress`, `model.CleanName`, and `model.LinkToken` with `NewLinkToken`, `ParseLinkToken`, and `Hash`; `model.Invitation`, `model.Member`, `model.SignInLinkCheck`, and `model.InvitationCheck`; and the lifetimes and the limit as constants. `model.User` gained `Role` and `InvitedBy`.

**Store** (`/sqlite/people.go`, migration `/sqlite/migrations/1795790000-people.up.sql`): a `role` column on `users` with a check constraint, `users.invited_by`, and new tables `sign_in_links` and `invitations`, which keep only the SHA-256 of each token. The template's `roles`, `users_roles`, `permissions`, `roles_permissions`, and `tokens` tables go, and so does `sqlite.Database.GetPermissions`, which glue's server no longer gets.

**Business logic** (`/service/people.go`), each operation with its wiring function in the existing pattern, and all of them in `service.Setup`, which now also takes the randomness and the first admin's address: `RequestSignInLink`, `SendSignInLink` (the job), `CheckSignInLink`, `SignIn`, `Invite`, `SendInvitation` (the job), `GetInvitations`, `WithdrawInvitation`, `CheckInvitation`, `AcceptInvitation`, `ListMembers`, and `ChangeRole`. The jobs in `/jobs/email.go` only decode and call the business logic. The old `login` email, with its log line that printed an address, is gone.

**Emails** (`/html/emails/`, `html.EmailTemplates`): glue's layout without the logo the app has no file for, in orange 700, and the bodies `sign-in.html` and `invitation.html`.

**HTTP** (`/http/people.go`, `/http/otel.go`): bare routes answering in plain text, `POST /sign-in`, `GET` and `POST /sign-in/link`, and `GET` and `POST /invitation`; `http.SignInSession`; a `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer` header on every page; and `http.HideQueryValues`, a wrapper round the tracer provider that `/cmd/app/main.go` installs before anything gets a tracer.

**Preview**: Markus is the admin, Signe and Thomas moderators, the rest members, all at example.com and invited by Markus. `preview.Outbox` keeps the newest hundred emails instead of sending them, and `/preview/emails` shows them with relative links that work. `/cmd/app/preview_test.go` checks the outbox's path, heading, and a log line too.

**Configuration**: `FIRST_ADMIN_EMAIL` and `BASE_URL` in `/.env.example`. The email sender's logger hides any value with an @ in it (`/cmd/app/log.go`).

**Tests**: against real SQLite for the store and the business logic; through the router for the bare routes, the session, and traces; a hand-written table for `model.Allowed` over the three roles, inactive, and nobody; hegel properties for addresses, names, and tokens; the fuzz targets `FuzzParseEmailAddress`, `FuzzCleanName`, `FuzzParseLinkToken`, and `FuzzHideQueryValues`; concurrent tries at what may happen once; and generated histories in the new package `/peopletest`, run by `TestFat_peopleHistory` in `/service/people_test.go` with 30 histories of 50 steps, and by the nightly "history" job through `HISTORY_CASES` and `HISTORY_STEPS`. New helpers: `jobstest.Take` and `jobstest.Run` for the job queue, `servicetest.Sender`, and `sqlitetest.CreatePerson`.

Decided by me, for the lead and Markus to overrule:

- One role per person is a column on `users`, not the template's tables, and the rights are a table in the code.
- The first admin's path is open only while `users` is empty, active or not. So changing `FIRST_ADMIN_EMAIL` later lets nobody in and demotes nobody.
- A sign-in link belongs to an address, not a person, so that the first admin can have one before they exist. Signing in uses up every link to the address.
- Asking checks only that the text is an address, which says nothing about membership, so "That is not an email address." is a refusal anyone can get. Everything about membership happens in the job.
- Tokens are 32 random bytes in URL-safe base64, 43 characters, in the query of the link: `/sign-in/link?token=…` and `/invitation?token=…`. A token is made in the job that sends it, so no raw token is stored, not even in the queue.
- An invitation's row is made at once, without a token, and its job makes the token. Withdrawing or replacing it before the job runs sends nothing.
- "At most 5 an hour" counts the links made for the address in the hour before. A link whose email failed is deleted and does not count.
- Addresses are trimmed, checked against the WHATWG rule HTML forms use (ASCII only), at most 254 bytes, and then lowercased, which is how they are stored and compared.
- Names follow the post body rule, on one line, and without the bidirectional controls, which would turn the text after a name around in a sentence or a subject.
- Inviting an inactive person's address is refused with `model.ErrorFormerMember`, since coming back belongs with moderation.
- An invitation keeps working if its sender stops being a moderator or admin, or is made inactive.
- Withdrawing an expired invitation is "not found", like one that is not there, and making an invitation deletes the expired ones.
- The list of members is in the order people joined.
- Signing in clears the session and gives it a new token, so whoever was signed in on that browser is signed out.
- The bare routes answer every link that does not work with 400 and one sentence: "This link does not work. Ask for a new one." and "This invitation does not work. Ask the person who invited you for a new one."
- Telemetry: spans per operation as before, with `user.id`, `invitation.id`, `role.target_user_id`, `role.from`, `role.to`, `sign_in.outcome`, `sign_in.joined`, and `invitation.outcome`; logs with IDs only.

The wording of the emails, for Markus:

It works once, for the next 15 minutes."; heading "Sign in to The Loft"; "Here is your link to sign in to The Loft. It works once, for the next 15 minutes."; a button "Sign in"; "If you did not ask for it, you can ignore this email. ### What I learned

- glue's `OpenTelemetry` middleware records `url.query`, and otelhttp `url.path`, before routing, and chi runs a group's middleware only for the routes that match in it. So a HEAD from a mail scanner, a 404, or a 405 put a live token in the trace. Overwriting the attribute from inside the router cannot reach those requests; wrapping the tracer provider does, since the SDK keeps the last value set for a key.
- glue's Postmark sender sends an HTML body only, with no text part. It logs the recipient on Postmark's 406, and Postmark's message, which can repeat the address, on 422.
- Every HTML page carries a new CSP nonce, so two pages are never the same byte for byte. The bare route answers in plain text; the page builder's page will differ only in the nonce.
- glue opens SQLite with `_txlock=immediate`, which is what makes a check inside a transaction safe against a second writer.

### What was tricky

- Keeping the answer to "send me a link" the same for everyone, which pushed every lookup into the job, and with it the making of tokens and the limit.
- The first admin, who needs a link before they exist, which is why links belong to addresses.
